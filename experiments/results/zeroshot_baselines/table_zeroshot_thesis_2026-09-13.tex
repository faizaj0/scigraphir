\subsection{Zero-Shot Generalisation}
\label{sec:generalisation}

\textbf{EQ3} tests Hypothesis~7.1: one model, trained on some fields, should
retrieve in fields it never saw. \textsc{SciGraphIR} is trained on two SIR-4
fields and tested on the other two, in both directions; models trained on
TOMATO-Star or on all of SIR-4 are tested on ResearchBench and MIR. Nothing
is tuned or selected on the target (Section~\ref{sec:zero-shot-transfer}),
and the baselines are training-free. Table~\ref{tab:zeroshot} reports nDCG@5 and the gap from
same-field to cross-field queries.

Within SIR-4 (Table~\ref{tab:zeroshot}a), the transferred model is best in six of the eight held-out
columns and has the smallest cross-field gap in both directions, about half
the dense baselines' gap when trained on Physics and Biology. On
ResearchBench and MIR (Table~\ref{tab:zeroshot}b), the model trained on all four SIR-4 fields is the best
system and matches the model trained on MIR itself
(Table~\ref{tab:main-results}); the TOMATO-Star model transfers less well,
below BM25 on ResearchBench and below Qwen3-Embedding on MIR. Differences
under one point (CS same-field, Biology cross-field) are within run-to-run
variation, and MIR tests a change of corpus rather than of field. The results
support Hypothesis~7.1: \textsc{SciGraphIR} transfers to unseen fields
without adaptation, and transfers best when trained on several fields.
